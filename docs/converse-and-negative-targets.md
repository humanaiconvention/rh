# Converse and negative targets: what would settle what?

**Useful negative results in this repository:** centering can reverse an uncentered sign inequality; prime regrouping can increase the diagonal; and a balanced convolution can preserve the entire unresolved history. These refute particular methods, not the desired estimate.

The concrete hypothesis here is

```math
\exists C\lt \infty\ \forall M\ge8:\quad |G(M)|\le C\sqrt M.
```

Its exact negation is

```math
\forall C\lt \infty\ \exists M\ge8:\quad |G(M)|/\sqrt M\gt C.
```

Equivalently, that ratio is unbounded. A power-law lower subsequence would suffice, but is stronger than necessary: subpower unbounded growth is another possibility.

## Three distinctions that prevent false counterexamples

1. **A zero cross term is not a zero mass.** From $`G^2=D_P+Y_P`$, $`Y_P=0`$ gives $`G^2=D_P`$; it does not give $`G=0`$. A nonzero cross term likewise does not establish growth.
2. **A large component is not a large complete expression.** A positive diagonal or one prime block can be compensated by the remaining signed terms. A lower-bound proof must retain the complete mass and all companions.
3. **Failure of this estimate is not automatically failure of RH.** Such an implication needs a separately proved equivalence with RH, including every analytic hypothesis. No such equivalence is established by this public extract.

The inverse bridge in the kernel note is a legitimate way to test an equivalent weighted family. It has a bounded inverse, so it preserves the estimation problem rather than producing cancellation for free.

A checkable next question, if the track is reopened, is to bound or make unbounded the **complete** normalized quantity $`|G(M)|/\sqrt M`$. The finite sign counterexamples already answer weaker negative questions. They do not resolve that one.

No specific theorem or quotation is attributed here to an external mathematician; this is the logical converse of the stated finite target.
